The player

In short
Profile

A three-position forward who has already played on three continents — and is ready for the next level.

Lodam Lee is a 2013-born, left-shot forward from Seoul, now at Kwangwoon Middle School. In three seasons he has taken seventeen international tournament entries across nine countries, for clubs and select teams from Minsk to Bangkok.

He plays center, wing and the point. That combination — real positional range plus genuine international mileage — is what makes him worth a roster spot at the next level, and what this page exists to show.

What he brings

Five ways to use him
01
For AAA & Tier 1 programmes

Roster flexibility you can use mid-game, not just on paper.

Most young forwards arrive as one thing — a center, or a winger. Lodam arrives as three. A coach can move him from the middle to the wall to the point without changing the lines, and can dress fewer specialists without losing a role.

In a tryout, that is not a small detail. It is one less decision a coaching staff has to make in March.

02
For coaches & scouts

A player who has already been tested away from home.

Every overseas prospect gets asked the same question: how does he handle unfamiliar systems, unfamiliar ice, unfamiliar opponents? Lodam has answered it seventeen times — CCM Selects in Slovenia and the Czech Republic, Vanbridge in Bolzano, Greater Bay in Hong Kong, the Bangkok Warriors in Thailand, Frostbiters in Malaysia, BY Minsk in Beijing.

What travels with him is not only skill. It is the habit of adapting quickly, and the composure that comes from having done it before.

03
For tournament & select-team invitations

Invite him, and you get a player who has already done this.

The risk with an overseas invitee is that he needs the first two games to find his feet. Lodam has been that invitee since 2022 — first with Chuncheon Little High1, then as a call-up for CCM Selects, Vanbridge Hockey, Greater Bay Lions, SWOT Hockey and the Bangkok Warriors.

He knows what it is to walk into a new room and learn new line-mates in a morning skate. For a short tournament, that is precisely the player a coach wants on the bench.

04
For sponsors & partners

A Korean pathway north — with a record already behind it.

Youth-hockey sponsorship usually buys goodwill. Here it buys a track record: seven seasons, seventeen international entries, nine countries, every one of them on film. A partner is not betting on potential alone — they are joining a player who has already proven he can compete outside Korea.

The next stage is specific: a North American season, AAA and Tier 1 tryouts, and the travel, training and equipment that come with them. What a partner gets is visibility with a player at the start of that climb — and a story that is straightforward to tell.

05
For the record

Every season, every tournament, on film.

The archive holds 94 videos — 19 of his own goals, 27 from Korean Ice Hockey Association events, 31 club games and 17 international tournaments. Nothing here is reconstructed from memory after the fact.

When a coach wants to see how he plays, they can, on this page, without leaving it.
